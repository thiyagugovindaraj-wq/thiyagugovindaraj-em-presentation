Problem Statement (PS):

1) Platform Stability:
Define reliability targets: SLIs/SLOs/SLAs — e.g., 99.9% API uptime, latency budgets. Mention error budgets as a way to balance feature velocity vs. stability.
Observability stack: CloudWatch dashboards/alarms, structured logging, distributed tracing — you can speak to this directly from your Lambda/API Gateway work at IDP.
Incident management: on-call rotations, severity levels, runbooks, blameless postmortems (5-whys), and closing the loop with action items.
Resilience patterns: retries with backoff, circuit breakers, dead-letter queues (you've done this with SNS/SQS DLQs), multi-AZ/VPC design, graceful degradation.
Proactive practices: load testing, chaos/failure injection, capacity planning ahead of peak traffic (e.g., enrollment season spikes for an education platform).
Sample story: "We built DLQ-backed messaging pipelines between microservices to guarantee zero message loss — reduced production incidents tied to dropped events by X%."
Asset Management

What interviewers are probing for: whether you can track and govern what the org owns — infra, licenses, tech debt — not just build features.

Cloud resource inventory: tagging strategy (owner, environment, cost center), using Terraform state as a source of truth for infra assets.
Lifecycle management: provisioning, decommissioning unused resources (stale S3 buckets, orphaned Lambdas, idle RDS instances) — ties directly to cost control.
Software/license management: tracking third-party libraries, SaaS subscriptions, and license renewals to avoid compliance gaps or shelfware.
Technical debt as an asset: maintaining a debt register, prioritizing remediation alongside feature work.
CMDB/documentation: architecture diagrams, service catalogs, ownership mapping so no service is a "bus factor of one."
Sample story: "Using Terraform as IaC gave us a single audit trail of every AWS resource — made quarterly access and cost reviews straightforward instead of manual discovery."
Cost

What interviewers are probing for: FinOps mindset — do you treat cloud spend as an engineering responsibility?

Visibility: cost allocation tags, AWS Cost Explorer/Budgets, per-service or per-team cost attribution.
Optimization levers: right-sizing compute, Lambda memory/timeout tuning, S3 lifecycle policies, Reserved Instances/Savings Plans for predictable workloads, spot instances for non-critical jobs.
Serverless cost angle: you can speak to this well — Lambda/API Gateway pay-per-use models naturally reduce idle spend vs. always-on servers.
Governance: budget alerts, monthly cost review cadence with engineering leads, killing zombie resources.
Trade-off framing: cost vs. reliability vs. speed — e.g., over-provisioning for safety margins costs money; show you can make that call deliberately, not by default.
Sample story: "Moved a workload to event-driven Lambda/SQS instead of always-on EC2, cutting compute cost by X% while improving elasticity during peak load."
AI

What interviewers are probing for: pragmatic, current thinking — not hype. As an EM, they want to know how you'll drive AI adoption responsibly.

Engineering productivity: AI coding assistants (Copilot-style tools) for code review, test generation, documentation — and how you'd measure actual impact (cycle time, PR turnaround) rather than assume value.
Product use cases: you already have a relevant example — the voice-enabled chatbot (speech-to-text/text-to-speech) you built at HCIndia. Frame that as early hands-on AI/NLP integration experience.
Governance & risk: data privacy when using AI tools (no sensitive data in prompts to third-party models), evaluating AI vendors' security posture, human-in-the-loop review for AI-generated code or content.
Team enablement: training the team on responsible AI tool usage, setting guardrails rather than blanket bans.
Where you'd apply it next: AI-assisted incident triage (summarizing logs/alerts), AI-assisted test coverage analysis, or using LLMs for support ticket categorization.
Sample story: "Built the audio chatbot's STT/TTS pipeline at HCIndia — taught me how to evaluate AI vendor APIs for accuracy, latency, and cost before committing to production use."
Vendor Management

What interviewers are probing for: can you manage external dependencies (SaaS, cloud, consulting) as strategically as internal teams?

Selection criteria: security/compliance posture, SLA guarantees, pricing model, support responsiveness, exit/portability options (avoiding lock-in).
Contract & SLA management: negotiating uptime guarantees, penalty clauses, renewal timelines; tracking vendor performance against SLA.
Relationship management: regular vendor review cadence, escalation paths, scorecards.
Risk mitigation: multi-vendor or fallback strategy for critical dependencies, understanding data ownership/portability in contracts.
Your AWS angle: even though AWS itself is the "vendor," you can talk about evaluating AWS managed services (Cognito vs. building your own auth, RDS vs. self-managed DB) as build-vs-buy vendor decisions.
Sample story: "Evaluated managed AWS services (Cognito, RDS) against self-hosted alternatives — weighed total cost of ownership and support burden, not just sticker price, before recommending a path."
